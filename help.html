How to Structure Your Narrative Structure

1. The Hook (130-150 words)

Provide the hook right away. Begin with the frame: “Today I’m going to talk about….” or something similar. Be sure to let the audience know what your normal life or mindset at the time of the story is to give them a baseline of where you, the storyteller, was at in your life.

2. Catalyst & Rising Action (400-450 words)

Introducing the decision/event/surprise that disrupted your life-as-you-knew-it. Build tension by walking your audience through the steps you took when faced with the unexpected. This will highlight how this affected you and/or risks involved.

3. The Climax / Peak (400-450 words)

Give the ‘Moment of Truth’ and describe the highest point of tension or the exact moment things went wrong (or right) or where you had to make a critical decision under pressure. Give good visual, emotional or physical details. This helps the audience feel like they are right there with you!

4. Resolution & Realization (250-300 words)

Show the immediate outcome or aftermath of that climatic event or choice and let them know the results or what happened. How did this experience change your behavior or shift your perspective? Explain the internal realizations.

5. The Takeaway (130-150 words)

Bring it all together in a personal insight into your truth. Try to connect your specific experience to your audience so they can potentially apply it to their lives, leaving them with a memorable closing thought.

Pro Writing Tips:

  • Use dialogue in your story instead of general comments. For example, instead of writing “I told him I felt weird…” write: “I looked at him and said, ‘I have a strange feeling in the pit of my stomach.’”
  • Be genuine with your experience and no detail is too small. Any little nugget that can draw your audience in is worth providing.
  • Remember above all else, this is YOUR story to tell.
